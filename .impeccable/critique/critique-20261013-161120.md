## Strong Evidence

- No horizontal overflow at the audited mobile and desktop widths across the
  homepage, survey, catalog, How It Works, Story, waitlist, privacy, and contact.
- Pages use one H1, coherent heading order, labeled form controls, meaningful
  image alternatives, intrinsic image dimensions, responsive WebP sources, and
  small progressive-enhancement scripts.
- The mobile navigation supports keyboard focus, Escape, focus restoration,
  and scroll lock. The survey exposes visible progress and preserves answers.
- The focused baseline completed 51 tests with seven expected skips.

## Findings

1. **P1 — Research signal mismatch.** The homepage asks visitors to react to
   six named Heritage Court directions, while the first survey question stored
   only broad categories such as Athletic and Casual. The journey could not
   measure the proposition it presented.
2. **P1 — Footer contrast.** The research contact link inherited `#005B73` on
   `#191919`, approximately 2.3:1, below WCAG 2.2 AA for normal text.
3. **P2 — Footer target size.** Footer and social links rendered near 21–26px
   high, including an especially narrow X target.
4. **P2 — Token conflict.** Runtime background/surface colors differed from
   both the approved brand contract and `DESIGN.md`.
5. **P2 — Reduced motion.** Broad descendant selectors forced `0.01ms`
   durations globally instead of disabling only the transitions and animations
   that exist.
6. **Release bookkeeping.** The four configured social URLs were still marked
   pending. The owner approved them during this review; X, YouTube, and TikTok
   resolved to TreadJoy accounts. Instagram exposed only its login wall.

The 7,315px mobile homepage is long but its survey action is already in the
hero and the six-look gallery appears early, so no structural rewrite was
recommended for this private-preview pass.

## Approved Direction

- Retain a five-step anonymous survey and replace Step 1 with six image-backed
  “most likely to wear” radio choices.
- Version responses in place so legacy style and price meanings remain
  auditable; refine current price bands around the $90–$120 hypothesis.
- Use private-preview responses only to validate comprehension and data
  capture, not to select a winning design.
- Restore the approved warm-stone/cool-surface tokens, correct footer contrast
  and targets, scope reduced-motion rules, and resolve the social content
  surface without claiming public-launch readiness.

## Tool Limitations and False Positives

- The bundled detector could not resolve Django `{% static %}` stylesheet tags
  and therefore emitted 40 repeated `rgb(0, 0, 0)` color advisories. Browser
  computed styles did not reproduce that drift; those advisories are treated
  as detector false positives.
- The browser harness did not expose Resource Timing/Performance APIs, so no
  Lighthouse, Web Vitals, or production performance claim is made.
